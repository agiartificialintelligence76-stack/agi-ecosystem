# Remaster briefs for the twenty new AGI-1 games

These briefs precede implementation. Each batch has a separate preview, live verification and report. Numbers in the briefs are design targets. Measurements belong in REMASTER-REPORT.md.

The four batches preserve the names and public URLs. Each uses the arcade’s native settings, local save namespace, accessible input, silent demo and Pop typography. The reference games establish the standard for interaction and verification, not a common template for gameplay.


## Music and listening

### DUET AFTER DARK (`echoforge`)

**Core decision**  Answer her sung phrase on the next bar. The pitch pads identify her notes, and the space between your notes earns the grade.

**Opening thirty seconds**  She sings two notes over the opening groove. Two lit pads invite an answer. The next call repeats the pitches with a different rest so you hear why timing matters.

**Five-minute arc**  Complete the first song, hear your ending grade and try a harmony answer in a contrasting tempo. Sections add pickups, held notes and rests.

**Thirty-minute arc**  Seven complete recordings total 22 minutes 59 seconds. Three arrangements per song, best grades and a rotating duet give another performance a different purpose.

**Mastery and return**  Keep the answer on the audio clock through syncopation and choose a harmony interval without losing the lead. The daily fixes a recording and part for everyone.

**Modes and progress**  Setlist, harmony/difficulty selection and daily duet. Save song and part grades locally.

**Motion and payoff**  Her mic passes the light to yours. Breath before the count, visual note tails, small confetti for a perfect answer and an audience wave at each chorus. Reduced motion keeps a steady spotlight.

**Sound**  Original recordings; three pitched voice syllables, count-in, answer onset, harmony bloom, restrained grade cadence and final applause. Music bus follows the heard audio clock.

**Balance and verification**  Hit-window boundaries; pitch versus timing; delayed input compensation; pause through a response; daily equality; full tracks on three skill profiles; silent demo. Aim for 45+ seconds to learn and 2–5 minutes to complete a song.

**Arcade comparison**  HEARTBEAT asks players to intercept notes in lanes. This duet alternates two performers. CABLE CHOIR gives unlimited time for recall, while the quality of this answer depends on its timing.

**AGI-1’s performance script**

- Opening — “my mic is warm. yours is next.” · “two notes. ready for the second one?” · “i'll take the count. you take the room.” · “listen to where i leave the silence.”
- Call — “that little breath belongs to the phrase.” · “my turn to make the first sound.” · “hear that pickup before the light?” · “i'm saving the long note for you.”
- Answer — “your mic, love.” · “sing right into the gap.” · “a little later on that last one.” · “hold the tail until my hand drops.”
- Harmony — “don't chase me. find the third.” · “there's room above my note.” · “you took the lower part. lovely.” · “we just made one chord with two voices.”
- Streak — “three answers without losing the pocket.” · “the band heard that one.” · “i stopped counting. we were already together.” · “that whole balcony just turned around.”
- Miss — “i came in early once too.” · “keep the next rest. we can catch up.” · “right note, a little ahead of my hand.” · “let the drum bring you home.”

### NEEDLE DROP (`returnaddress`)

**Core decision**  Skate through three grooves pressed into a turning record, jump the needle damage and physically back-cue the music to recover a passage.

**Opening thirty seconds**  A star rides toward her skate in the middle lane. A second star pulls her outward. A conspicuous coral scratch introduces the first jump with enough room to miss safely.

**Five-minute arc**  Skate one full side, earn a grade, choose another recording. Inner and outer groove transitions, grouped stars and scratches differ with each arrangement.

**Thirty-minute arc**  Ride seven complete original tracks. Tour moves through the set, practice isolates a side and the date selects a pressing. Sleeve grades and a full-combo stamp remain on the shelf.

**Mastery and return**  Move before the change of groove and jump over scratches at the audible beat. A rewind restores score, needle damage and the playing audio to the same downbeat. Saved bests reward a clean run over farming rewinds.

**Modes and progress**  Tour, practice by song and daily pressing; normal and harder cuts. Return for an unmarked sleeve or a fresh daily layout.

**Motion and payoff**  The vinyl grooves actually rotate, the skater leans into a change, wheels spray on a perfect pickup, scratches lift the deck arm, and a gold ring sweeps out at a chorus.

**Sound**  Source MP3s on Web Audio, quiet wheels, jump, landing, missed star, skip, backspin transient, chorus bell and end chord. Restore actual source offset after rewind.

**Balance and verification**  Measured beat-grid extraction, playable lane transitions, jump clearance, elapsed audio versus update clock, rollback restoring score exactly once, mute/pause/cancel and simulated time-to-failure. Newbie median ≥45 s, steady 120–300 s, expert clearly longer in Tour.

**Arcade comparison**  It shares songs with HEARTBEAT and SIDE B. Here the player continuously positions a skater and can wind back a physical record. None of the other 51 games combine spatial groove changes, jumps and reversible playback.

- Jump — “that scratch goes right across the record.” · “knees up. let it pass.” · “heels clear. keep rolling.” · “i could feel the bass under that jump.”
- Streak — “four clean wheels. one clean line.” · “every star in that bar was ours.” · “hear the crowd on the downbeat?” · “my wheels are practically singing.”
- Mistake — “that star's coming round in my dreams.” · “a little scrape. i'm still up.” · “bent the needle. spare me another.” · “next groove. let that one go.”
- Backspin — “hold the vinyl. take us back.” · “same downbeat. different landing.” · “i love a second chance in stereo.” · “last backspin. make it a good one.”

### CABLE CHOIR (`summitline`)

**Core decision**  Remember the order of the singing cabins and play it back. At higher stations, remember notes sounding together as a chord.

**Opening thirty seconds**  A cabin sings from the left, a second answers across the mountain, and the player answers at their own pace. The baton clearly changes hands.

**Five-minute arc**  Ride through a series of short melodic landmarks. Each adds a note, changes register, introduces a chord, or removes a visual hint.

**Thirty-minute arc**  Learn a repertoire of original melodies and increasing variations, then climb an endless memory ladder and compare the longest sequence on the daily song. A local songbook records discoveries.

**Mastery and return**  Hear interval groups as phrases; recognize where a chord belongs inside a long call. Untimed response keeps mental recall separate from reaction speed.

**Modes and progress**  Concert book, endless climb and date-seeded melody; unlock stations and melody badges.

**Motion and payoff**  Cabins sway on their cable as they sing; the conductor cues each with her baton. Chords bring two cabins together in light. The entire valley answers at each summit.

**Sound**  Distinct tuned cabin bells, warm bass drone, wind, conductor count, simultaneous chord with headroom, wrong bell, summit cadence. Give each pitched shape a matching visible cue.

**Balance and verification**  Seed manifests, repeatable daily phrases, chord recognition, free response tempo, solvable repertoire, measured polyphonic peak and ascending skill-profile medians.

**Arcade comparison**  DUET AFTER DARK tests precise timing. This choir holds the next bar for the player. It is a sequence-memory game with harmonic groups; it has no music lanes, discs, faders, or grappling paths.

### SIDE B (`tidepool`)

**Core decision**  Cue a real record in headphones and crossfade it onto the air at a musically useful moment while following the listener’s energy request.

**Opening thirty seconds**  One deck is already on air. Two sleeves offer different energies. A bouncing cue lamp shows when to bring in the second deck and the sound actually changes as the fader moves.

**Five-minute arc**  Fill a side with several mixes. Learn downbeat, phrase and energy separately, then time an incoming record before the request expires.

**Thirty-minute arc**  Run a late shift across the seven full tracks, unlock cuts, respond to a stream of requests and collect signatures on the mixtape box. Each seed changes the order of requests and available sleeves.

**Mastery and return**  Read the outgoing phrase, audition the incoming downbeat, preserve headroom while both are live, and set up the next request before the crowd changes mood.

**Modes and progress**  Hosted shift, open-ended late shift, daily request schedule. Keep best blends and edit stamps.

**Motion and payoff**  Two independent reels wind, VU meters respond to each deck, fader visibly travels across, listener postcards turn as requests land, stamp the sleeve after a strong blend.

**Sound**  Seven real songs, independent gains on one audio clock, timed equal-power fades with master headroom, cue tap, transport click, slip cue, request chime and end-of-side latch.

**Balance and verification**  Envelope endpoints and combined maximum, playback alignment and fallback, no duplicate sources across pause/retry, request scoring, seeded music order, performance by three DJ profiles.

**Arcade comparison**  The player is the programmer of a continuous radio set. The comparison games use recordings as material to perform to or skate on; SIDE B asks which recording goes next and how its beginning meets the outgoing song.

### ABYSS FM (`pearlshift`)

**Core decision**  Listen to a chorus underwater and triangulate one animal by its call, bearing and depth. Decide whether the remaining pings will resolve competing candidates.

**Opening thirty seconds**  A single voice calls from one side. A visible directional trace duplicates the cue. Move the hydrophone, spend a ping and mark where the two bearings meet.

**Five-minute arc**  Identify a moving singer while a second call crosses its track. Choose a receiver depth to hear through the first thermocline. Catalog the correct animal and hear its complete call.

**Thirty-minute arc**  Travel through survey regions with distinct acoustics, layered calls, displaced readings, moving targets and progressively tighter ping budgets. Return to fill the hydrophone log or face a seeded night survey.

**Mastery and return**  Estimate motion while the sensor is moving, hold a bearing in memory and choose the second receiver position to maximize separation. The log rewards exact identification with few transmissions.

**Modes and progress**  Calm survey and timed night shift, repeatable daily water, local species log.

**Motion and payoff**  Live oscilloscope, fading bearings, moving receiver, growing uncertainty ellipse on a weak fix, a surfacing silhouette and a page turning for a new species.

**Sound**  Distinct synthesized call signatures, stereo pan by bearing, filtering by distance and layer, hydrophone drone, ping, thermocline static, clean lock and field-log stamp with a visual equivalent for every clue.

**Balance and verification**  Two-receiver geometry, parallel or degenerate bearings, moving targets, depth bias, panning/mono visual accessibility, seeded identification, peak with simultaneous calls. Calibrate calm completion and three timed accuracy profiles.

**Arcade comparison**  It is an inference game, not an aim-and-fire game. Unlike CANDID CAMERA's focus, the unknown is a moving sound source behind a layer; unlike either concert title, reproducing notes earns nothing.

### Grid pack foundations

All five keep their existing names, URLs and local save keys. These are design and acceptance targets. The level lists are individual authoring commissions. The eventual pack contains a literal, reviewed layout or stage script for each, a replayable witness, and the measured shortest par. Do not count reflected layouts, seeds, or tutorial variants toward the campaign total.

The first input teaches the first verb. Open directly on a playable board with one indicated target. A later world introduces one unknown rule in isolation, gives the player a second example with a different solution, and only then combines it with known rules. Each has its own interface. A common shell handles pause, settings and statistics. Live controls stay in the DOM, work with focus and keyboard, and have at least 44 CSS pixels of target area. Board gestures have a button or keyboard equivalent. At 360 px text stays at least 12 CSS pixels. Portrait puts the board above its controls. Landscape moves the controls beside the board. The foldable and desktop layouts can show the next objective and world map without reducing the board. Reduced motion keeps the positional, symbol and text answers while disabling shake, flashes and hit-stop. Full-size boards use procedural Pop artwork, with the kit's ink, sticker shadows and self-hosted AK fonts.

Plan the 20-minute content check from first-time human playthroughs. Record first input, world reached, first independently solved example and time, along with whether the player can explain the verb. A perfect solver's runtime is a proof, not a retention measurement. Distinguish modelled times from human playtest timings in release evidence. In demo, set a representative mid-world situation with its real controls and real simulation, then land a legible payoff within 5.7–11.7 seconds. Demo and hints use pure rules. End demo loops without saving, loading external art or counting a play.

### HANDLE WITH CARE · waxworks

**Mental model and core verb.** AGI-1 rolls a two-cell-tall parcel from one edge to its next. It stands on one tile or rests on two. Lying sideways can bridge a gap that standing on it cannot. The scale accepts only an upright parcel with the required postage. The route is about turning the parcel into the correct shape before reaching a bottleneck.

**First 30 seconds.** Show a long box on its side, a scale two rolls away and AGI-1 bracing her shoulder against the short end. Tapping the highlighted edge tips it. A footprint sticker shows the two cells it will cover next. The first seal arrives with a rubber stamp and her handwritten initials. The next job asks the player to turn a corner before standing up.

**Five minutes and thirty minutes.** By five minutes the player has a page of landing stamps and has learned to cross glass sideways. At thirty minutes the stamp office separates the parcel into two units, asks the player to choose one to roll, and reunites them at a matching seam. Finishing any 5 of a world's 8 depots opens the next world. Remaining jobs and precise pars can be revisited from an illustrated routing ledger.

### LAST TRAIN TO NOODLES · switchyard

**Mental model and core verb.** Work a miniature railway shunting yard by coupling wagons and routing the locomotive around them. Each bowl ticket specifies the order in which its ingredient cars must cross the kitchen exit. The facing direction of the engine and the length, order and accessible end of every consist matter. A turnout is a piece of track the player can set; cars cannot pass through one another.

**First 30 seconds.** AGI-1 leans out of her four-wheel switcher. Tap a highlighted platform and the engine rolls to the next berth. A real Couple control lights beside the broth car. One joined movement crosses the kitchen stripe and the first bowl appears with visible steam. The next ticket shows broth before egg while the two cars begin in the opposite order. A short siding supplies the answer by doing.

**Five and thirty minutes.** Five minutes earns the first six station seals and teaches how to leave a car on a siding. At thirty minutes a player is running the engine around the back of a consist, rotating a single car and preparing complete recipes with multiple exits in view. A yard stays available after each solve so the player can cut needless run-arounds. Four of six tickets open the next line.

**Thirty-six handmade yards, six per line.**

| Line and new rule | Individual yards |
